Wilderness First Aid / Control Severe Bleeding

  1. Expose the wound and press firmly with gauze or clean cloth.
  2. Maintain pressure and add material without repeatedly lifting the first pad to check.
  3. For uncontrolled life-threatening limb bleeding, apply a commercial tourniquet according to training and record the time.

Do not loosen a tourniquet after application; obtain emergency evacuation as quickly as possible.

Primary sources: American Red Cross; NOAA National Weather Service

Survival Basics / Decide Whether to Stay or Move

  1. Ask whether anyone knows your route and whether searchers are likely.
  2. Compare the risks of staying with the terrain, distance, daylight, weather, injury, and certainty of any move.
  3. If staying, make shelter and signals; if moving, leave a clear note and mark direction and time.

Do not move on a guess or separate a group without a clear rescue reason.

Primary sources: U.S. National Park Service

Find and Treat Water / Choose the Safest Source

  1. Prefer a known safe supply, then clearer moving water collected upstream from camps, livestock, roads, or waste.
  2. Avoid water with fuel sheen, chemical smell, toxic algae, dead animals, mine drainage, or industrial runoff.
  3. Collect where the bank is stable and you cannot be swept into the water.

Boiling and ordinary filters cannot make chemically contaminated or salty water safe.

Primary sources: Centers for Disease Control and Prevention

Wilderness First Aid / Treat Hypothermia and Frostbite

  1. Move out of wind and wet, replace wet clothing, and insulate above and below.
  2. Warm the chest, neck, armpits, and groin with wrapped warm packs or shared body heat.
  3. Protect frostbitten tissue from pressure and refreezing; evacuate serious cases.

Do not rub frostbite, use direct fire, give alcohol, or thaw tissue that may refreeze.

Primary sources: American Red Cross; NOAA National Weather Service

The useful details.

Does Aurora work without a connection?

Yes. Once the selected model and map packages are prepared, Aurora’s guidance, Manual, maps, and species scanner run locally.

How is Aurora’s content verified?

Manual content carries source metadata and exact passage links, map packages are signed before activation, and the current build was validated on a physical iPhone 13.

Which devices are supported?

Aurora is being built for iPhone and iPad.

Can I download it now?

Not yet. Aurora Survival is coming to the App Store. Contact Aurora for product and partnership conversations.
